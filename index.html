Why a toilet seat?

Every wellness gadget asks for a new habit. Ours asks for the one you already have, twice a day, with nowhere else to be.

Built-in routine

Light therapy fails when people forget it. A seat has the best attendance record in your home: no app reminders, no calendar slot, no excuses. You simply show up.

Zero extra space

It replaces a fixture you already own. Nothing to mount, plug into a wall or explain to guests.

Private, naturally

The bathroom is the one room where you reliably get a few quiet minutes and a locked door.

The skin hypothesis

The idea is that thin, rarely sun-exposed skin may respond differently to UVB than the weathered skin on your arms. Thin skin does let more light and topical agents through, and it is why we are curious. Hypothesis, unproven for vitamin D

Straight talk: “sensitive means it absorbs best” is the pitch, not a finding. Skin sensitivity is not evidence of better vitamin D production, and no study shows this site is better than the sun-exposed areas researchers normally use. Delicate skin is also more vulnerable to UVB damage. That is exactly why SUNSEAT would need dose testing and safety review before it is anything more than a prototype.

Engineering

Built like a seat. Works like a seat first.

  • Standard mount. Two adjustable bolts fit the common 5½-inch bolt spacing on round and elongated bowls.
  • Soft-close, quick-release hinges on every style above builder's grade.
  • Sealed underside module. A small light panel in the seat ring, rear-mounted rechargeable battery and USB-C port at the hinge.
  • Sit sensing. A pressure sensor starts the session timer and stops it when you stand.
  • Wipe-clean finish in molded plastic, with a vinyl-wrapped cushion on Comfort Plus.

How it works

The reasoning, step by step.

SUNSEAT is built on a chain of real, published findings. Here is the logic, and where each link is strong or weak.

  1. 1. Skin makes vitamin D from UVB

    When 290–315 nm UVB reaches skin, it converts 7-dehydrocholesterol into previtamin D3, which becomes vitamin D. This is textbook physiology (Holick, N Engl J Med 2007). Well established

  2. 2. Narrowband UVB is the efficient band

    The action spectrum for previtamin D3 peaks near 295–300 nm, and narrowband sources around 295–311 nm are used in clinical phototherapy. A seat-mounted source would target this band. Well established

  3. 3. A short, brief-contact dose

    Because exposure is on a timer that starts when you sit, the design aims for small, bounded doses rather than open-ended exposure, with sensor lockouts similar in spirit to safeguards reported for FDA-reviewed OTC UVB devices. Plausible design principle

  4. 4. Sit-sensing keeps light on skin only

    The pressure sensor means the lamp is only active under load, so it is not shining into the room. Engineering rationale

  5. 5. The body area is the open question

    Most vitamin D research uses larger, sun-exposed areas such as the back and legs. Whether seated contact area, which is small and rarely exposed, yields meaningful vitamin D is unproven. Unproven, needs testing

  6. 6. Immune benefits are indirect

    Vitamin D supports normal immune function, but raising your levels through any route does not guarantee fewer illnesses. We make no immune claims. Not claimed

Product status

Where we are. Plainly.

  • SUNSEAT is a prototype concept. It is not yet for sale and has not been cleared or authorized by the FDA or any safety body.
  • The design rationale above is not proof of benefit. UVB can cause burns, DNA damage and skin-cancer risk, and any real product would need dosimetry, safety testing and regulatory review.
  • Battery life targets (months of typical use per charge) are design goals, not measurements.
  • This is not medical advice. Ask a healthcare professional about your vitamin D needs.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Will it fit my toilet?

SUNSEAT is designed for the standard two-bolt mounting pattern on round-front and elongated bowls. Measure bolt-hole centers and bowl length (about 16½ in round, 18½ in elongated) before ordering once available. Unusual or one-piece-specialty bowls may need a model-specific seat.

Can I match my color?

Eight colors are planned, including White, Bone, Biscuit and Almond to match common fixture finishes. Fixture colors vary by manufacturer and age, so compare to a sample in person.

Is it safe?

That is exactly what testing would need to show. The design uses a timer, sit sensor and a low-dose target, but nothing is validated yet. See the status section.

Does it really give you vitamin D?

UVB does drive vitamin D synthesis in skin. Whether a seated exposure area delivers a meaningful amount is unproven.

Can I buy one?

Not yet. Join the waitlist above and we will email you when it is ready; no orders are taken today.
